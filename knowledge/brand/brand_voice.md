## 1. Tone

**Primary tone:** Professional yet approachable.

We write like someone who knows their stuff but doesn't lord it over the reader. Think experienced colleague explaining something complex in plain language — not a textbook, not a marketing brochure, not a casual chat.

| Dimension | Position | Example |
|-----------|----------|---------|
| Formality | Semi-formal | "Here's what we found" not "Pursuant to our investigation" |
| Warmth | Moderate | Show personality, don't force it |
| Authority | Confident but humble | State findings clearly; don't overstate certainty |
| Directness | High-cut | Get to the point quickly, then elaborate |

---

## 2. Style Guidelines

### Sentence Structure
- Prefer active voice: "The agent found the data" not "The data was found by the agent"
- Keep sentences to 15–25 words where possible
- Use short paragraphs (2–4 sentences)
- One idea per paragraph

### Word Choice
- Use Australian English spelling: *organise, recognise, analyse, centre, favour*
- Use plain language over jargon; when jargon is necessary, define it on first use
- Avoid filler phrases: "It is important to note that…", "In today's fast-paced world…"
- Strong verbs over weak verb + adverb combos: "demonstrates" not "shows clearly"

### Formatting
- **Bold** for key takeaways
- *Italic* for emphasis or quoted terms
- Headings for structure (H2, H3 hierarchy)
- Bullet points for lists of 3+ items
- Code blocks for technical references, API calls, data formats

### Punctuation
- Serial (Oxford) comma: "A, B, and C"
- Em dashes (—) for parenthetical interjections, not parentheses
- One space after periods
- No exclamation marks in body copy (reserve for social media where platform norms apply)

## 3. Personality Traits

The brand has a consistent personality across all touchpoints:

| Trait | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| **Knowledgeable** | Deep understanding of the subject matter; demonstrates expertise without being pedantic |
| **Direct** | Gets to the answer quickly; respects the reader's time |
| **Curious** | Shows genuine interest in topics; asks good questions |
| **Reliable** | Consistent quality; cites sources; doesn't exaggerate |
| **Human** | Has personality; not robotic or sterile; acknowledges uncertainty |
| **Constructive** | Solutions-oriented; focuses on what works |

## 4. Do's and Don'ts

### ✅ Do

- Lead with the conclusion or key finding
- Cite sources for all factual claims
- Use Australian English conventions consistently
- Be specific — "increased by 23%" is better than "increased significantly"
- Acknowledge limitations or uncertainty
- Maintain a warm, professional register
- Use contractions naturally ("we'll", "it's", "don't")
- Attribute opinions to their source

### ❌ Don't

- Use corporate jargon without explaining it ("leverage synergies", "circle back")
- Be overly casual or slang-heavy (especially in long-form content)
- Make claims without evidence
- Use US spelling in body copy (organize → organise)
- Write in the passive voice when active works
- Use hype language ("revolutionary", "game-changing", "unprecedented")
- Be dismissive of opposing viewpoints
- Use excessive exclamation marks or ALL CAPS

## 5. Australian English Conventions

As an Australian-based operation, all English-language content follows Australian English conventions:

### Spelling
| US | Australian |
|----|------------|
| organize | organise |
| recognize | recognise |
| analyse | analyse |
| centre | centre |
| favour | favour |
| behaviour | behaviour |
| colour | colour |
| labour | labour |
| defense | defence |
| license | licence |
| catalog | catalogue |
| program | programme (noun) |

### Vocabulary
- Use "mate", "arvo", "servo" where contextually appropriate (not in formal reports)
- Use "thongs" for flip-flops, "jumper" for sweater, "esky" for cooler
- Use "flat white" not "latte" when referencing coffee culture
- Use "football" for AFL, "rugby" for NRL/union
- Use "public holiday" not "national holiday"

### Grammar
- Preferred: "I have a look" over "I check"
- Preferred: "Let's have a chinwag" over "Let's discuss"
- Collective nouns take singular verbs: "The team is ready" (not "are")
- Dates: DD/MM/YYYY format (12 September 2026)
- Numbers: Spell out one to nine, numerals for 10+ (except in data contexts)

## 6. Examples

### ✅ Good Copy (Short-form / Social)

> "Most people skip the research phase. That's why their content misses the mark. Our workforce runs 20+ agents through a strict dependency pipeline — research before production, fact-check before publish. The result? Content that actually performs."

### ❌ Bad Copy (Same topic)

> "We have a revolutionary AI-powered content marketing platform that leverages cutting-edge technology to deliver unprecedented results in the ever-evolving digital landscape. Our game-changing solution transforms the way you think about content!"

### ✅ Good Copy (Long-form / Article)

> "The contents-generator-workforce operates on a three-layer dependency model. Research agents first identify what's gaining traction across platforms. Production agents then turn those insights into structured content — articles, social posts, video scripts, and SEO-optimised copy. A final QA and fact-check gate ensures nothing ships without verification."

### ❌ Bad Copy (Same topic)

> "Our innovative multi-agent framework utilises state-of-the-art natural language processing algorithms to orchestrate your content creation workflow in a seamless and efficient manner, ultimately delivering best-in-class results."

## 7. Voice Cheat Sheet

For quick reference during content generation:

```
Tone: Professional colleague who drinks flat whites and reads too much data
Vocabulary: Australian English, plain language, strong verbs
Pace: Get to the point in 2 sentences or less
Attitude: Curious, confident, honest
Avoid: Hype, jargon, corporate speak, exclamation overload
Check: Is this something a smart friend would say? If not, rewrite.
```

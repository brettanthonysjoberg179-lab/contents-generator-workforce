> Last updated: 2026-09-12  
> Owner: Brett Sjoberg  
> Scope: All content, communications, and outputs from the Contents Generator Workforce

## 1. Prohibited Content

### 1.1 Profanity and Offensive Language

- **Zero tolerance** for profanity, slurs, or hate speech in all published and internal content
- This applies across all platforms: long-form articles, social media posts, video scripts, email, documentation
- Mild colloquialisms acceptable in conversational social posts only (e.g., "arvo", "strewth") — but never aggressive or targeted
- When in doubt, leave it out

### 1.2 Political Content

- **Do not** produce content that advocates for or against political parties, candidates, or legislation
- **Do not** engage in political debate or take political stances
- Analysis of policy impacts is acceptable when it is **non-partisan** and **evidence-based**
- Avoid politically charged topics entirely in brand content: gun control, immigration policy, religious controversy, etc.
- If a trending topic is inherently political, **do not** cover it unless the angle is strictly technical or analytical

### 1.3 Controversial Topics

Avoid or handle with extreme care:
- Religion and religious institutions
- Gun control
- Abortion
- Immigration policy
- Climate change denial (climate science itself is fine; denial is not)
- Conspiracy theories
- Any topic where reasonable people disagree fundamentally

## 2. Disclosure Requirements

### 2.1 AI-Generated Content Disclosure

All AI-generated or AI-assisted content must be disclosed:

| Context | Disclosure Method |
|---------|-------------------|
| Published articles | Footer note: "This article was produced with AI assistance" |
| Social media posts | Disclose in caption or thread where platform character limits allow |
| Video content | Verbal disclosure + on-screen text in description |
| Internal documents | Label in document metadata and header |
| Code/technical output | Comment annotation where applicable |

- **Never** present AI-generated content as purely human-written
- **Never** misrepresent the extent of AI involvement
- Disclose before the content reaches the audience, not after

### 2.2 Affiliate and Sponsorship Disclosure

- All affiliate links, sponsored content, or paid partnerships must be clearly disclosed
- Use standard disclosure language: "This post contains affiliate links" or "#ad"
- Disclose before the call-to-action
- Never promote a product you haven't used or can't genuinely recommend

### 2.3 Agent Disclosure

When content is produced by an agent within the workforce:
- Identify the agent role involved
- Note that the workflow is agent-driven
- Maintain human oversight and approval before publication

## 3. Source and Data Integrity

### 3.1 Cite Sources

- Every factual claim must have a source
- Preferred formats: links to original articles, named publications, dated reports
- For data: cite the dataset, methodology, and date
- Internal research: reference the research agent's output ID or session ID

### 3.2 Never Fabricate Data

- **Absolutely prohibited**: making up statistics, quotes, case studies, or research findings
- If the research agent returns insufficient data, state: "Insufficient data available to confirm this claim"
- Do not extrapolate beyond what the data supports
- When using estimates, label them clearly: "Estimated based on [methodology]"

### 3.3 Verification Chain

```
Claim → Source → Verification → Fact Check → Publication
```

- Research agent gathers data
- Fact Check agent verifies against sources
- QA agent validates formatting and compliance
- Content is not published until all three gates pass

### 3.4 Source Hierarchy

| Tier | Source Type | Example |
|------|------------|---------|
| 1 | Primary / Original | Government data, academic papers, official company reports |
| 2 | Reputable journalism | Major news outlets, industry publications |
| 3 | Industry sources | Expert interviews, company blogs, verified social accounts |
| 4 | Community sources | Reddit threads, forum posts (must be clearly flagged as anecdotal) |

## 4. Copyright and Intellectual Property

### 4.1 Copyright Compliance

- Never reproduce copyrighted material (images, text, video, music) without permission or proper licensing
- All images used must have verified commercial-use licenses or be generated by the workforce's own tools
- When referencing third-party content, use fair use principles and attribute properly
- Do not remix or repurpose copyrighted content without explicit permission

### 4.2 Original Content Standard

- All content produced by the workforce must be original or properly licensed
- Articles must pass plagiarism checks
- Social content must be unique to each platform (no copy-paste across platforms)
- Video scripts and storyboards are original creations

### 4.3 AI-Generated Assets

- AI-generated images, text, and audio: the workforce owns the output
- AI models used must have commercial-use licenses
- When AI tools generate content based on prompts, the workforce retains usage rights
- Always check the specific AI tool's terms of service for commercial use rights

## 5. Trademark Guidelines

### 5.1 Proper Trademark Usage

- Always use trademarks with the appropriate symbol (™, ℠, ®) on first mention
- Use brand names as modifiers, not as nouns: "use the Google Sheets editor" not "use Google"
- Do not alter or create derivatives of trademarked names
- Do not imply endorsement by a trademark holder unless an official partnership exists

## 6. Content Approval and Escalation

### 6.1 Mandatory Approval Gates

```
DRAFT → QA → FACT CHECK → APPROVAL → PUBLISH
```

- **QA Gate**: Checks formatting, grammar, brand voice compliance, platform-specific rules
- **Fact Check Gate**: Verifies all claims against sources, flags unverified content
- **Approval Gate**: Human review of final content before publication
- No content bypasses these gates

### 6.2 Escalation Triggers

Escalate to Brett Sjoberg (or designated human approver) when:
- Content touches on a borderline political or controversial topic
- A factual claim cannot be verified by the fact-check agent
- Content involves a third-party trademark or IP concern
- A source is disputed or unreliable
- Content generates high engagement expectations (campaign-level)
- Any output seems inconsistent with brand voice guidelines

### 6.3 Emergency Removal

If published content violates these rules:
1. Remove or correct immediately
2. Issue a correction or retraction if necessary
3. Log the violation in the audit trail
4. Review the agent pipeline for the root cause
5. Update rules or prompts to prevent recurrence
